The thumbnail analyzer correctly categorized 16 of the 20 test inputs and incorrectly categorized 4, resulting in an overall accuracy of 80 percent. Most correct results occurred when thumbnails had qualities, such as large readable text, strong contrast, clear subjects, or layouts. The incorrect results shared a feature: they were close to the boundary between two categories. Some average thumbnails contained one strong design element that raised their score into the effective category. Another thumbnail had bold text but did not clearly show the product, causing it to score lower than expected. These differences occurred because a thumbnail can contain both effective and ineffective qualities at the same time. The analyzer considered the complete design and title relationship, while the expected score sometimes emphasized a single visual feature. More precise category definitions and borderline examples would make future expected results more consistent and improve the reliability of the experiment.
